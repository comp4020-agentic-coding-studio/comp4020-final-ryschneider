# Crit 8 reflection

**What surprised me about the propagation/fade rule once it was running.** I imagined fade as a simple timer on each mark: it appears, it dims, it's gone. Running it showed that the interesting question isn't how fast a mark fades but whom it has reached. A mark that spreads only to the readers present when it was written disappears from the experience of anyone who arrives a minute later, so the page quietly turns into a record of who was online. Once marks extend with every viewer, the heat starts to behave like attention in a room rather than like a log, and the fade constants matter far less than I'd assumed. I also hadn't expected how much rendering granularity would change the feel. The same data reads as a sharp underline at one scale and as a mood at another.

**Where I redirected the agent, and what made me catch it.** Three places. The agent fixed a mark's carrier set at write time, and I caught it by picturing a late joiner: they'd see none of the heat the group had built. It also wanted to auto-seed a demo room so the deployed site wouldn't look empty, and I caught that by asking what production code would be doing to flatter a first visit. Then there was the seeding script's import problem, where the fix I wanted was to share the real module rather than copy the rule into the script. In each case the tell was the same: something that worked for the next ten minutes and would be wrong in a week.

**What I'm deliberately leaving for next week.** Accounts beyond a display name, moderation, mobile layout, and several texts per club. I'm also leaving the fade constants alone. They're tuned by eye and I'd rather watch real use before fitting them to anything. None of this is time I ran out of; I left it out so this week's question stays narrow.

**What I'd warn a friend about.** Decide what a mark means before you decide how it looks. I lost the most time to rendering questions that were really unanswered product questions, like who a mark reaches and whether it outlives its author's presence. If you settle those first, the visual and the transport choices mostly make themselves.
